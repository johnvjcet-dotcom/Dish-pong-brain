How the culture learns

The spot where the ball will arrive, relative to the paddle, is stimulated onto 16 electrodes on the left. Spikes from the hidden layer reach two groups of motor neurons on the right, and the difference in their firing rates moves the paddle.

A hit is followed by a regular sweep of pulses, a predictable pattern. A miss is followed by random bursts that scramble the synapses, more strongly while the culture is still doing badly. Synapses whose spikes came before a good move get stronger, and synapse totals per neuron stay balanced.

This is a small simplified model, not a recreation of the lab experiment. Besides hit and miss, it also gets a distance error signal while the ball approaches, which is what makes it learn within minutes. As the culture gets good, its background noise and learning rate fade, which is why long streaks appear later.
